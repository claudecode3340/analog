## Q1. Research-scientist vs ML/CV-engineer tracks: degree requirements, MS vs PhD vs working first, and 2025–2026 visa/immigration conditions

### Takeaway
At frontier labs, research-scientist (RS) roles still ask for a PhD "or equivalent practical experience". Engineer roles do not. US immigration got much more expensive and uncertain in 2025–2026:
- the $100K H-1B fee (now blocked by a court but extended by a new proclamation)
- a wage-weighted lottery
- a proposed $70K/$30K OPT fee

The route that suffers least is an F-1 → OPT → H-1B change of status inside the US, but that pipeline itself is now under threat. Europe (ELLIS, Germany, Switzerland) and the UK PhD (3-year post-study work) look relatively more attractive.

### Inferences
- **RS vs engineer.** For RS roles at FAIR, GDM and similar labs, the PhD is the default filter. "Equivalent practical experience" in practice means first-author papers at top venues (CVPR/ICCV/ECCV/NeurIPS/ICLR). ML/CV engineer and research-engineer roles take BS/MS holders with strong systems and engineering skills.
- **Thesis vs course-based MS.** A thesis MS (or IISc MTech Res / IIT MS(R)) produces papers and strong letters, which matter for PhD admission. A course-based US MS is mainly an immigration and job pipeline (F-1 → OPT → H-1B change of status). That pipeline faces the proposed OPT fee and the wage-weighted lottery, where new grads at wage Level I get the fewest entries.
- **US funding.** A funded US PhD (RA/TA stipend, tuition waived) avoids debt. A US PhD → H-1B change of status still escapes the $100K fee, and PhD-level research salaries usually sit at higher wage levels, which gets more lottery weight. So a US PhD is less hurt by the 2025–26 changes than an MS → job plan. This is my inference, not a cited policy analysis.
- **Working first.** A year or two as a pre-doc or RA in India (GDM India, MSR India, IISc/IIT labs) costs nothing and avoids taking on debt now under unstable US rules. It also produces publications that strengthen PhD applications.
- **Immediate deadlines.** The ELLIS deadline (31 Oct 2026) is two weeks away. The UK's 2-year Graduate Route ends for applications after 31 Dec 2026, which only matters for people finishing a UK degree now.

## Q2. India-specific routes and how they feed into PhD admissions abroad

### Takeaway
The strongest India-based stepping stones are:
- Google DeepMind India's 24-month pre-doctoral researcher role
- MSR India's Research Fellow programme (1–2 years, for BTech/MTech graduates; PhD holders not eligible)
- IISc/IIT research degrees and RA roles
- summer fellowships (IASc-INSA-NASI SRFP; IIT Delhi SRF)

Each produces publications and strong letters, which are what PhD committees abroad weigh most.

### Cited Findings
- **Google DeepMind India pre-doctoral researcher.** A 24-month fixed-term role in which you work with GDM India scientists before deciding on a PhD. Pre-docs are expected to publish externally. The 2025-start application asked for a CV, an English transcript and a statement of purpose of at most 500 words, with a deadline of 18 Dec 2024. A "2026 Starts" variant appears on aggregators; a 2027-start posting and deadline were not confirmed — [powertofly mirror](https://powertofly.com/jobs/detail/2069528); [theindiajobs mirror](https://jobs.theindiajobs.com/c/Google-Careers/jobs/pre-doctoral-researcher-university-graduate-2025-starts-fixed-term-contract-rbeah5zkyb)
- **MSR India Research Fellows.** Open to people with a BS/BE/BTech or MS/ME/MTech in CS or related areas who have graduated by the time they join. Aimed at those within about 3 years of graduating. PhD students and holders are not eligible, international applicants are accepted, and the term is 1–2 years. Selection looks at academics, "raw smarts", development skills and research aptitude. This is distinct from the global Microsoft Research Fellowship for PhD students (deadline 2 Dec 2025) — [MSR India RF page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/research-fellows-program-at-microsoft-rese); [MSR Fellowship 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/2026-microsoft-research-fellowship/)
- **Google Student Researcher 2026.** A listing exists (mainly for current BS/MS/PhD students) — [Google careers](https://google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/103667844620657350-student-researcher-2026)
- **IASc–INSA–NASI Summer Research Fellowship (SRFP).** Two-month fellowships with a 65%-in-core-subjects eligibility threshold. The SRFP 2027 form reportedly closes 30 Nov 2026 (aggregator; verify on the academy portal). Reported dates for the 2026 cycle conflict — [talentd](https://www.talentd.in/articles/science-academies-summer-research-fellowship); [biotecnika](https://www.biotecnika.org/2025/11/summer-research-fellowship-2026/)
- **IIT Delhi Summer Research Fellowship 2026.** Applications ran 16 Mar–3 Apr 2026, with work from 13 May–13 Jul 2026 — [IIT Delhi SRF](https://oldacademics.iitd.ac.in/srf/main.php)

### Inferences
- **Feeder logic.** One or two years as a pre-doc or RF, with one or two top-venue papers and letters from well-known researchers, is the most common way strong Indian applicants get into top US/EU PhD programmes. This is widely reported anecdotally but not cited here.
- **Timing.** A 4th-year student in Oct 2026 should check now whether GDM India and MSR India RF applications for 2027 starts are open (the GDM cycle has historically closed in December). SRFP 2027 closes around 30 Nov 2026.
- **IISc MTech (Research) / PhD and IIT MS(R)/PhD.** These are admitted via GATE or interviews, with an MoE/HTRA stipend. IIIT-Hyderabad CVIT (CV group) admits to MS-by-research and PhD and is a strong CV-specific option in India. [not re-verified in this session]
- **Industry research internships in India** (Adobe Research India, Samsung R&D Bangalore, Sony Research India) are typically for current students and are a lighter-weight credential than a pre-doc. [not re-verified]

## Q3. Current best resources (2025–2026 editions) and GPU access in India

### Takeaway
- **CS231n.** The Spring 2026 offering (Fei-Fei Li, Ehsan Adeli, Justin Johnson et al.) has numpy-heavy Python assignments worth 45% of the grade. Its 2026 lecture videos are Stanford-only; earlier years are on YouTube.
- **UDL.** Prince's book is free online with a maintained notebook repo (chapters 1–21).
- **GPU access in India.** The IndiaAI compute portal offers subsidised GPUs at roughly ₹65–67 per GPU-hour. Students register with an APAAR ID.

### Cited Findings

  - Assignments: in Python with numpy, worth 45% of the grade.

### Inferences (resource map built around the three books) [all URLs and edition details below not re-verified this session]
**ISLP (James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani, Taylor, 2023)**
- Free PDF and Python labs at statlearning.com, using the `ISLP` pip package.
- Do it first, or in parallel in months 1–2, as the classical-ML and statistics foundation: regression, classification, resampling, regularisation, trees, SVMs, unsupervised learning.
- Milestone: re-implement the ch. 6 (regularisation) and ch. 8 (trees) labs from scratch, then a Kaggle tabular competition.

**UDL (Prince 2023, free, with notebooks)**
- The core DL theory spine, months 2–5.
- Do every chapter notebook, and pair it with CS231n assignments (numpy backprop, CNNs, transformers, generative models).
- Bishop & Bishop, "Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts" (Springer 2024, free online at bishopbook.com), is an alternative or complementary probabilistic treatment.
- Mathematics for Machine Learning (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong; mml-book.github.io) is for math gaps.
- d2l.ai (Dive into Deep Learning, PyTorch edition) covers code-first practice.

**Szeliski 2nd ed. (2022, free at szeliski.org/Book)**
- Classical and geometric CV, months 3–8: image formation, features, alignment, structure from motion, stereo, 3D reconstruction, NeRF/neural rendering.
- Pair with CMU 16-385 (UG CV) / 16-720 (grad CV) assignments, MIT 6.8300 (Advances in CV), or Berkeley CS280.
- Milestones: implement Harris/SIFT matching → homography plus RANSAC panorama stitcher → two-view SfM / an eight-point fundamental matrix → a simple NeRF or Gaussian-splat reproduction.

**Engineering skill**
- PyTorch official tutorials (pytorch.org/tutorials).
- Karpathy's "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" (micrograd → makemore → GPT), nanoGPT and llm.c, for building from scratch and performance intuition.
- fast.ai Practical Deep Learning, for top-down applied practice.
- Hugging Face courses (LLM/NLP, diffusion, CV, deep RL) for current-ecosystem tooling.
- CS224n (Stanford NLP), for transformers and language, since modern CV is increasingly multimodal (vision-language models).

**Research capstone (months 9–12)**
- Reproduce a recent CVPR/ICCV paper with the official code, run ablations, and write it up as a workshop paper or arXiv report. This is the artifact pre-doc, RF and PhD committees read.

**Free compute**
- Google Colab (free T4, time-limited); Kaggle Notebooks (around 30 GPU-hours per week historically); Lightning AI Studio free monthly credits.
- IndiaAI portal for bigger jobs.
- Cloud education credits (AWS, GCP, Azure for Students), whose terms vary.

**Efficient learning from textbooks (brief; evidence summary from background knowledge, not cited here)**
- Retrieval practice (testing effect) and spaced repetition beat re-reading.
- Interleave problem types, and do end-of-chapter problems before looking at solutions.
- Use deliberate practice: work at the edge of your ability, get fast feedback through unit tests and gradient checks, and target your weak spots.
- Practical rule: read a section, close the book, re-derive or re-implement it, then add 3–5 flashcards for the key equations. Tie each chapter to a small coding deliverable.
