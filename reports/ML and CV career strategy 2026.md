# Ship Deployable Vision Systems, Not Certificates

The evidence as of October 2026 points one way. A 4th-year Indian ECE/EEE student who starts with almost no Python can become a hireable ML/CV engineer within 12–18 months. The way to do it is to aim at **perception and edge-CV engineering roles**: build Python/PyTorch fluency and deep-learning fundamentals first, then two or three original projects that hiring managers can probe. Those projects should cover (a) a real-data vision model with rigorous evaluation, deployed and quantised on constrained hardware, and (b) camera geometry with some C++. An "AI/ML" credential stack, Kaggle-only work or tutorial clones will not do the same job. The reason is the market. AI-skill demand is growing fast: AI-skill postings rose **+55% in the US in 2025** (Lightcast, Apr 2026) and India's AI/ML postings rose **+45% over FY26** (Naukri, Mar 2026). That growth sits inside a tech market that hires fewer juniors. **New grads were only 7% of Big Tech hires in 2024**, more than 50% below 2019 (SignalFire, May 2025). Only **~3% of US ML-engineer postings are explicitly entry-level** (365 Data Science, Apr 2026). In India, **56% of GCC hiring targets 4–10 years of experience** (Jul 2026). So the bar is "proof of work", not a degree. Among real 2025–26 postings, Python and PyTorch are near-universal. The things that separate perception and edge roles are **C++, TensorRT/ONNX/quantisation and multi-view geometry, each appearing in 6 of 11 fully read postings**. Technology trends point the same way. Frozen foundation models such as DINOv3 and SAM 3 now commoditise "train a detector". Verified deployment, evaluation design, data engines and geometry-plus-learning stay scarce. The research-scientist track still gates on a PhD. For this student, a research career is best reached through India's pre-doc and research-fellow programmes (Google DeepMind India, MSR India) and then a funded PhD. A self-funded US MS has become riskier: a $100K H-1B fee is now court-blocked but has been extended, a wage-weighted lottery started in 2026, and a $70K OPT fee was proposed on 7–8 Oct 2026. The three books the student owns map well onto this plan. ISLP covers months 1–4, Prince is the deep-learning spine for months 2–8, and Szeliski is the geometry and imaging engine for months 4–11. The ECE background (DSP, image processing, communications) is a real advantage, because it lines up with Szeliski chapters 2–3 and with edge and hardware-adjacent employers.

## AI hiring grows inside a smaller, more senior tech market

### Globally, AI-skill demand is booming while junior tech hiring shrinks

The direction of the market is well established. The size of the AI share is not. The Stanford AI Index 2026 (Lightcast data, published 13 Apr 2026) found that **2.5% of all US postings mentioned AI skills in 2025, up 55% on 2024**. Python was the top specialised skill, appearing in 258,674 postings, up nearly 30% ([Lightcast](https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/stanford-ai-2026)). Indeed's tracker measured a much higher share, a record **4.2% of US postings in Dec 2025**. On Indeed, AI-mentioning postings rose more than 130% during 2025, but **total tech postings were still ~34% below Feb 2020** ([Indeed Hiring Lab, 22 Jan 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/01/22/january-labor-market-update-jobs-mentioning-ai-are-growing-amid-broader-hiring-weakness/)). A vendor using broader keywords reports ~19% ([FrogHire, Q1 2026](https://www.froghire.ai/report/ai-readiness)). Lightcast, Indeed and FrogHire therefore disagree by up to 8x on the level, purely because they define an "AI job" differently. Quote each figure with its source rather than as "X% of jobs are AI". Indeed adds a nuance that matters for this student: **"traditional AI/ML roles in data and computer science declined after peaking in early 2022"**. The growth is AI spreading as a skill into many jobs, and the new "AI engineer" title, rather than more classic ML jobs ([Indeed, Jan 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/01/22/january-labor-market-update-jobs-mentioning-ai-are-growing-amid-broader-hiring-weakness/)). LinkedIn's Jobs on the Rise 2026 (Jan 2026) ranks "AI engineer" #1 in the US ([letsdatascience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/linkedin-names-fastest-growing-jobs-for-2026-bf17881e)). The best ML-specific signal is from mid-2025 and is secondary: **ML-engineer postings stood at an index of 159 against 51 for general software engineers (Feb 2020 = 100, Jul 2025)** ([Pin.com citing Indeed](https://www.pin.com/blog/tech-job-market-report/)). The UK shows the same pattern. Specialist AI postings rose 61% in 2025 while total vacancies fell 6.6%, though the source, PwC, sells AI services ([PwC, 2026](https://www.pwc.co.uk/press-room/press-releases/research-commentary/2026/ai-jobs-barometer-2026.html)).

The new-grad picture is the part that should shape the plan. SignalFire (20 May 2025, 2023–24 data) found that **new grads were 7% of Big Tech hires, down 25% from 2023 and more than 50% from 2019**. At startups they were under 6%. Mid- and senior-level hiring did recover in 2024 ([SignalFire](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025)). Stanford's Digital Economy Lab (Aug 2025, ADP payroll data) found a **13% relative employment decline for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations**, software development among them. Older workers in the same jobs were flat or growing. The authors call this early evidence, not proof of causation ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/)). Layoffs continued into 2026. Q1 2026 had 81,700 tech layoffs, the worst quarter since early 2023 ([KRON4, May 2026](https://www.kron4.com/news/technology-ai/number-of-tech-layoffs-in-2026-already-tops-100k-report/amp/)). The trackers disagree badly on totals. For 2025, layoffs.fyi counts 122,606 and Value Add VC counts 205,773. For 2026 to roughly August, they count ~125.8k and ~205.8k ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/tech-layoffs-2026-surpass-2025-total)). Read the evidence together. Postings for experienced ML engineers grew, while junior pipelines narrowed, plausibly because employers combine AI tools with experienced hires. A new grad therefore has to look like a low-risk hire who already does junior-engineer work.

### In India, GCCs drive growth, but freshers get the smaller slice

India's numbers are stronger but follow the same shape. Naukri JobSpeak shows AI/ML postings **+49% YoY in Feb 2026, +45% for FY26, slowing to +22% in May 2026**. Fresher (0–3 years) hiring grew more slowly and also decelerated: +17% in Feb 2026, falling to +7% in May 2026 ([Naukri, Feb 2026](https://www.naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-it-hiring-shows-meaningful-recovery-and-ai-momentum-continues-white-collar-market-posts-12-yoy-growth-in-february-2026/amp); [Naukri, May 2026](https://www.naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-may-2026-white-collar-hiring-remains-stable-in-may-ai-ml-roles-and-insurance-sector-continue-to-lead/amp/)). Within AI/ML, the fastest-growing band was **₹50 LPA+ roles (+55%, Mar 2026)**. That band is senior, not fresher ([Naukri, Mar 2026](https://www.naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-march-26-records-a-9-rise-in-white-collar-hiring-as-fy26-closes-at-8-the-strongest-job-growth-in-three-years/), snippet only). Global Capability Centres (GCCs) are the engine. Nasscom–Zinnov counts **2,117 GCCs with ~2.36M employees in FY26**, and AI/data/analytics is their fastest-growing function at **+38% YoY** ([Zinnov, May 2026](https://zinnov.com/centers-of-excellence/zinnov-nasscom-india-gcc-landscape-2026-report/)). GCCs hired 227,991 people in H1 2026 (+11%) while overall white-collar hiring fell 9% in June. **Nearly two in three new GCC roles ask for AI, data or automation skills**. However, **56% of GCC hiring targets 4–10 years of experience**, and "early career" is only about 30% ([Swarajya citing Business Today, 2 Jul 2026](https://swarajyamag.com/economy/ai-data-skills-now-drive-two-in-three-new-gcc-jobs-in-india-as-hiring-rises-11-per-cent)). Staffing firms report large skill gaps. TeamLease (Sep 2026) estimates a GenAI supply gap of ~53% and says only ~16% of IT professionals have AI skills, and it calls these figures directional ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/talent-gap-genai-cloud-india-stands-53-60-teamlease-digital-report)). A skill gap is not a pile of open fresher seats. It means employers struggle to find people who can *demonstrate* the skill, which is exactly what a portfolio fixes. Geographically, Bengaluru takes 25–30% of AI and GCC hiring, and Hyderabad is the fastest-growing Tier-1 city ([CBRE via Careers360, Dec 2025](https://news.careers360.com/bengaluru-delhi-ncr-mumbai-dominate-ai-job-openings-on-naukaricom-70pc-share-cbre/amp); [foundit via Eastern Mirror](https://www.easternmirrornagaland.com/hiring-surges-across-india-as-ai-linked-jobs-rise-exponentially)). IT-services fresher volumes are flat for FY27, at TCS ~25,000 and Infosys ~20,000 ([Business Today, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/tcs-fresher-hiring-hits-25000-in-fy27-more-depends-on-demand-525337-2026-04-13); [AngelOne](https://uatcdn.angelone.in/news/stocks/infosys-share-price-down-over-5-fresher-hiring-plan-stands-at-20-000-for-fy27)).

Fresher ML/CV roles in India rarely carry the title "ML Engineer – fresher". The visible entry routes are internships that convert to jobs (pre-placement offers made up ~24% of IIT Delhi's Phase-1 offers in 2025–26), campus SWE/systems tracks at semiconductor GCCs, research-fellow and pre-doc programmes, and occasional small-company CV roles ([CollegeDost](https://collegedost.in/blog/engineering-colleges/iit-delhi-placements/)). Here is a concrete open example. Recrew AI posted a **Computer Vision Engineer role for 0–2 years in Bangalore on 16 Oct 2026**. It is open to Electronics and Physics graduates, accepts PyTorch "via coursework or personal projects", and pays ₹6–8 LPA ([jobfound](https://jobfound.org/job/recrew-ai-is-hiring-for-computer-vision-engineer-bangalore-urban-karnataka-india-india-16-october-2026)). The CV and deep-learning postings found at NVIDIA India and Qualcomm were mostly senior, or asked for an MS plus years of experience ([BuiltIn Bengaluru](https://builtinbengaluru.in/job/senior-software-engineer-deep-learning-and-computer-vision/7613691); [Bayt](https://www.bayt.com/en/india/jobs/lead-engineer-senior-computer-vision-74881221/)). A Qualcomm "Engineer, AI & ML Systems" role in Hyderabad did accept BTech plus 1 year ([beBee](https://bebee.com/in/jobs/engineer-ai-ml-systems-qualcomm-hyderabad-telangana--theirstack-665774217)).

## Postings require Python and PyTorch everywhere; perception roles add C++, deployment and geometry

The research team read 11 ML/CV postings from 2025–26 in full and catalogued 35 more from snippets. In the fully read set, **PyTorch appears in 9/11 and Python in 8/11**. Four more skill clusters each appear in **6/11**: C++, TensorRT, quantisation/pruning/distillation, and multi-view geometry/SLAM/state estimation. **Data curation, labelling and evaluation also appear in 6/11**, almost always as "preferred". CUDA appears in 4/11, never as required. Publications appear in 4/11, also never required. **Docker, Kubernetes, MLflow and distributed training appear in zero** of the perception and new-grad postings read in full. This is a convenience sample of 11 postings, not a statistical estimate. Broader Glassdoor-based counts (365 Data Science, article dated 23 Apr 2026, collection window not stated) support it. Across US ML-engineer postings they find **Python ~72%, PyTorch 42%, TensorFlow 34%, Kubernetes 15%, Docker 12%, MLflow 5%, and computer vision ~15%**. Degree requirements in the same data are **PhD 36%, MS 22%, BS 18%**, and only **~3% of postings are entry-level** ([365 Data Science MLE](https://365datascience.com/career-advice/career-guides/machine-learning-engineer-job-outlook-2025/)). The same analysis of "AI engineer" postings finds LangChain at ~11% and RAG at ~14% ([365 Data Science AI engineer](https://365datascience.com/career-advice/career-guides/ai-engineer-job-outlook-2025/)).

Two patterns drive the plan. The first is that **the deployment cluster (TensorRT, ONNX, quantisation, CUDA) is "preferred" in new-grad postings and "required" in mid-level ones** (Anduril, Wayve). It is therefore the most realistic differentiator a new grad can show, because it moves a candidate from "has trained models" to "can do the junior job". The second is that postings which accept bachelor's candidates spell out what counts as evidence. Bot Auto counts neural-net training experience from **"coursework, research, personal projects, open-source work, and internships"**, with emphasis on debugging failing models. True Anomaly asks for the ability to **read and implement ICRA/IROS/CVPR papers** and offers C++ training. NVIDIA's NCG inference role accepts **"class projects, internships, research, or open-source work"** ([Bot Auto](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/botauto/jobs/5371947008); [True Anomaly](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/trueanomalyinc/jobs/5221970007); [NVIDIA](https://jobs.anitab.org/companies/nvidia/jobs/65287558-ai-software-engineer-llm-inference-performance-analysis-new-college-grad-2026)). None of the postings read says Kaggle results count. Big-company "new grad" ML titles in the sample, such as Waymo VLM and NVIDIA inference, required an MS or PhD, and Waymo's even listed 5+ years of experience. That supports focusing on startups, mid-size firms and Indian GCCs for a bachelor's-level first job.

## Foundation models commoditise model training; deployment, evaluation and geometry stay scarce

Between 2024 and 2026, general-purpose vision models changed what a CV engineer actually does. Meta's **DINOv3 (Aug 2025)** is self-supervised on 1.7B images at up to 7B parameters. Meta claims that "a single frozen vision backbone outperforms specialized solutions" on dense tasks, which is a vendor claim ([Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/blog/dinov3-self-supervised-vision-model/)). **SAM 3 (19 Nov 2025)** segments *every* instance that matches a text phrase in images and video. Ultralytics, Roboflow and Voxel51 integrated it within weeks ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/what-is-sam3/)). Open VLMs such as **Qwen3-VL (23 Sep 2025, Apache-2.0)** claim Gemini-2.5-Pro-level perception, though Simon Willison notes the benchmarks are self-reported ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Sep/23/qwen3-vl)). The industrial pattern is to use foundation models as frozen feature extractors and auto-labellers, then **distil into a small detector that meets a latency budget**. Roboflow's Autodistill packages "label with Grounding DINO, then train YOLO" as one command ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/autodistill/)). Auto-labels still fail on dense and fine-grained scenes, such as stocked retail coolers and multi-class trash, so humans and data-engine design stay in the loop ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/ai-vs-human-labeled-data/)).

3D vision is moving from hand-built pipelines to geometry-aware learning. **VGGT won CVPR 2025 Best Paper**. A single feed-forward transformer infers cameras, depth and point maps from one to hundreds of views in under a second. Its successor VGGT-Ω is a CVPR 2026 best-paper finalist whose authors flag possible benchmark contamination in an earlier checkpoint ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Wang_VGGT_Visual_Geometry_Grounded_Transformer_CVPR_2025_paper.html); [LearnOpenCV](https://learnopencv.com/vggt-vs-vggt-%cf%89-vggt-omega-a-complete-guide-to-feed-forward-3d-reconstruction/)). Gaussian splats entered industrial standards when the glTF extension KHR_gaussian_splatting reached release candidate on 3 Feb 2026 ([Informed Infrastructure](https://informedinfrastructure.com/post/khronos-announces-gltf-gaussian-splatting-extension)). Autonomous driving has largely converged on end-to-end learning (Tesla, Wayve). Wayve raised $1.2B in Feb 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/self-driving-tech-startup-wayve-raises-1-2b-from-nvidia-uber-and-three-automakers)). Embodied AI (VLAs such as π0–π0.7 and GR00T N1, and world models such as Genie 3 and V-JEPA 2) is the hottest research area, but hiring concentrates in a few well-funded labs ([HF/NVIDIA](https://www.huggingface.co/blog/nvidia-physical-ai); [TechCrunch, 5 Aug 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/05/deepmind-reveals-genie-3-a-world-model-that-could-be-the-key-to-reaching-agi)).

Inference cost now drives a great deal of engineering, and the details are where skill shows. NVIDIA measured FP8 TensorRT shrinking a CLIP image encoder by 48% and making it 1.39x faster ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/model-quantization-turn-fp8-checkpoints-into-high-performance-inference-engines-with-nvidia-tensorrt/)). A 2026 edge study tells the other side. On Jetson-class hardware, **FP8 lost 3–5% accuracy against 0.2–0.4% for FP16, and requesting FP4 without explicit Q/DQ nodes silently produced FP16 kernels** ([arXiv 2608.14770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14770)). Calling an export function is commoditised. Proving what actually runs, and at what accuracy, is not. Evaluation is the cross-cutting scarce skill, a point Chip Huyen stresses ("the more AI is used… the more important evaluation becomes", opinion, Dec 2024/Jan 2025) ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-ai-engineering-stack)). Karpathy (opinion, Oct 2025) calls coding agents useful but immature ([CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news-letter/karpathy-says-ai-agents-are-a-decade-out/)). Boilerplate PyTorch is therefore being automated, while judgement, verification and debugging are not.

| Tier (synthesis from the evidence above) | Skills | Implication for the plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Commoditised** | Training a standard classifier or detector on a curated dataset; default fine-tuning/LoRA; zero-shot VLM prompting; manual labelling; one-click ONNX/INT8 export; boilerplate training code | Never make one of these a portfolio centrepiece |
| **Durable** | Linear algebra, probability, optimisation; DL fundamentals deep enough to implement papers; camera models and multi-view geometry; debugging and experiment design; production software engineering | The books cover exactly these; do not skip the "boring" chapters |
| **Scarce** | Verified low-precision/edge deployment and profiling; evaluation design (in-domain test sets, failure slices, contamination checks); data engines (auto-label QA, active learning, synthetic data); geometry combined with learning (feed-forward recon, SLAM fusion); robot learning/VLA; kernels and large-scale serving | Projects 1–2 target the first four; kernels and VLA are later options, not the first job |

The tier assignments are this report's inference from technology evidence and postings. No labour-market series measures them directly. Confidence is high for the commoditised and durable tiers and medium for the ranking within the scarce tier. Of the scarce skills, **efficiency and evaluation transfer to every sub-field and carry the least risk**. Robotics and humanoids have the highest upside and the highest risk, because the capital is concentrated, weighted toward China, and deployment is unproven.

## Defensible projects and SWE-level coding decide new-grad ML/CV interviews

No rigorous survey measures how much hiring managers weight projects, open source, Kaggle or papers for new grads. The research notes found almost none, and the open web is dominated by vendors selling prep. So this section rests on practitioners' opinions, and those opinions agree with each other. The consistent message is **two or three original, end-to-end projects defended in depth, rather than many shallow ones**. Recruiters recognise course-template repos, and MNIST, Titanic and Iris read as "followed a tutorial" ([Upskillist, 2026](https://www.upskillist.com/blog/10-ai-portfolio-examples-impress-recruiters/), vendor opinion). A wrapper around a hosted model "doesn't count" ([Medium, 2025](https://medium.com/@santosh.rout.cr7/ml-engineer-portfolio-projects-that-will-get-you-hired-in-2025-d1f2e20d6c79), opinion). Yuan Meng, an MLE at DoorDash, writes that "nothing is worse than making a random decision without any justifications". MLE loops often include a dedicated **project deep-dive** covering alternatives, trade-offs, challenges and impact ([Yuan Meng, 15 Oct 2024](https://www.yuan-meng.com/posts/mle_interviews/)). Eugene Yan's advice to go "off the happy path" makes the same point: your own messy data surfaces the bugs that teach you, and those are what interviewers ask about ([mrdbourke.com](https://www.mrdbourke.com/get-a-machine-learning-job/)). Blogging helps as a way to *share* strong work. Rachel Thomas knew of blog posts that led to job offers, but called branding "no substitute for doing high-quality technical work" ([fast.ai, 2017](https://www.fast.ai/posts/2017-12-18-personal-brand.html)). Kaggle has moved from rank-as-credential to public notebook as work sample. Only top finishes carry real weight ([GeeksforGeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/machine-learning/is-kaggle-useful-in-finding-a-machine-learning-job/), opinion). For CV in particular, the equivalent of a tutorial clone is "fine-tune YOLO on a Roboflow dataset". Projects that touch real cameras, geometry, latency budgets or quantisation are rarer and line up with the preferred-skill clusters above. That is an inference, not a measured finding.

The 2024–26 MLE loop, as practitioners describe it, has six parts:
1. Data-structures-and-algorithms coding at the software-engineer bar. Meta asks two Mediums in 45 minutes, and elsewhere one Hard plus one Medium is common. Poor coding "can easily disqualify a candidate".
2. ML coding: k-means, logistic regression by gradient descent, **multi-head attention**, metrics, or debugging a PyTorch snippet.
3. ML system design, a 45–60-minute "mini design review". Chip Huyen says almost every company asks it and candidates find it hardest.
4. ML breadth: bias–variance, regularisation, backprop, optimisers.
5. ML depth in the candidate's domain.
6. Behavioural questions; MLEpath suggests preparing about 8 stories.

Sources: [Yuan Meng](https://www.yuan-meng.com/posts/mle_interviews/); [MLEpath, 23 Dec 2024](https://mlepath.substack.com/p/pass-ml-engineer-interviews-in-2025); [Huyen, ML Interviews book](https://huyenchip.com/ml-interviews-book/), written around 2020–21. CV loops add classical geometry. Glassdoor reports from Mujin, a robotics-vision company (~2024), describe questions on **essential vs fundamental vs homography matrices, camera intrinsics, lens distortion, and how intrinsics change after cropping**, plus a design question on estimating an object's pose. One reviewer saw no ML questions at all ([Glassdoor Mujin](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Mujin-Interview-E1097457-RVW94976435.htm)). AI-assisted coding has split formats. Meta began rolling out an **AI-enabled coding round in October 2025**, alongside a classic no-AI round. Candidates must "use AI, but… show you understand the code" ([Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/meta-ai-enabled-coding), secondary; not confirmed for ML loops specifically). Proctored platforms ban AI outright ([TechScreen](https://techscreen.app/articles/is-using-ai-during-coding-interview-cheating)). In-person rounds reportedly rose from 24% (2022) to 38% (2025) ([Interview Query](https://interviewquery.com/p/ai-interview-trends-tech-hiring-2025), snippet only). Taken together, live explanation of your own work now counts for more than polished take-home output. That favours a candidate who built and debugged their projects personally rather than prompting them into existence.

For Indian candidates trying to reach international or GCC research teams, the verifiable structured routes are the **Google DeepMind India Pre-Doctoral Researcher** role and the **MSR India Research Fellows** programme. The GDM role is 24 months in Bangalore. It requires a STEM bachelor's, Python/C++/Java and "some ML/AI research experience", prefers a master's, and expects external publication ([The Muse](https://themuse.com/jobs/google/predoctoral-researcher-university-graduate-2025-starts-fixedterm-contract)). The MSR programme runs 1–2 years for BTech/MTech graduates. It requires at least one recommendation letter, allows department heads to nominate up to three final-year students per institute, and does not take PhD holders ([MSR RF nominations](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/research-fellows-program-at-microsoft-research-india/nominations/)). Abroad summer internships are unreliable this year. One guide reports Mitacs Globalink and DAAD WISE *suspended* for Indian students in 2026 ([IITian Vibes](https://iitianvibes.com/blog/iit-summer-internships-2026-mitacs-daad-shut-and-2027-map/)). Others still list 2027 deadlines ([LeapScholar](https://leapscholar.com/blog/research-internship/)). All of these are consultancy blogs, so check the official pages. The notes found **no data** on how effective referrals or cold outreach are for Indian candidates.

## Engineer first; reach research through Indian pre-docs, because US routes got costlier

Research-scientist roles at frontier labs still set the minimum at a PhD "or equivalent practical experience". Examples include Meta's Research Scientist, Computer Vision (2025, $117K–173K plus equity) and Google DeepMind's Research Scientist, Gemini Vision (copy dated 1 Oct 2026) ([Meta](https://metacareers.com/jobs/1047447520078379); [AnitaB/GDM](https://jobs.anitab.org/companies/google-2-6e248a84-5b9b-4601-aff7-7c2e9e285b9c/jobs/95467056-research-scientist-gemini-vision-deepmind)). Engineer and research-engineer roles take BS and MS holders with strong systems skills. **The evidence therefore supports an engineer-first plan that keeps a research door open, not a research-first plan.**

The trade-offs follow from this. A **self-funded, course-based US MS starting in 2027** now carries debt into a pipeline that faces a possible $70K OPT cost, a lottery that disfavours entry-level wages, and live litigation. It is no longer the default good bet, though it is not closed. A **funded PhD** (US or Europe) is less exposed. There is no tuition debt, an in-country change of status avoids the $100K fee, and research salaries sit at higher wage levels. That last point is the notes' inference, not a cited policy analysis. Europe (ELLIS, Germany, Switzerland) and UK PhDs, which keep 3-year post-study work, look relatively better than they did two years ago. In India, three routes feed directly into PhD admissions and cost nothing:
- GDM India pre-doc and MSR India RF;
- research degrees at IISc or the IITs (MTech Research, MS(R), PhD);
- IIIT-Hyderabad's CVIT group, a strong CV-specific option.

The notes did not re-verify admission calendars, stipends or GATE 2027 dates for these, so check them directly. **Recommendation:** decide the research-vs-engineer question at month 11 using evidence about yourself (see the plan's decision gate), not now. Skip ELLIS 2026: two weeks remain, there is no research record, and the letters would be weak. The realistic research timeline is pre-doc/RF applications in late 2027 and PhD applications in the 2028–29 cycle.

## An 18-month plan from zero Python to deployed, geometry-aware vision work

This plan is built backwards from the evidence. Python and PyTorch fluency plus DL fundamentals come first, because they are the floor in 9/11 postings and the ML-coding round tests them from scratch. Project 1 targets the deployment and evaluation/data-engine clusters, which are the top "preferred" differentiators and scarce skills. Project 2 targets geometry and C++, because those separate perception roles from generic ML and CV interviews test them directly. Project 3 is chosen at a decision gate and targets either open source/edge (engineer) or a paper reproduction (research). Interview preparation runs throughout, because coding at the SWE bar can disqualify a candidate on its own.

**Planning assumptions (not facts; adjust them):**
- About 15–18 focused hours a week until graduation, assumed to be around May–June 2027.
- About 30–35 hours a week afterwards if the student is not employed, or 10–12 if employed.
- Free GPUs from Colab and Kaggle, then the IndiaAI compute portal (~₹65–67/GPU-hour, students register with an APAAR ID; [CareerIndia](https://www.careerindia.com/news/indiaai-mission-2026-access-subsidized-gpus-boost-ai-careers-scale-startups-011-66885.html), secondary) for larger jobs.

**What to drop if behind:** cut in this order: ISLP 7 and 9, Szeliski 10, 13 and 14, and Prince 14–18. The following are **never cut**: Prince 2–12, CS231n assignments 1–2, the deployment half of Project 1, the geometry core of Project 2, and LeetCode practice.

### The three portfolio projects, and why each matches the evidence

**Project 1, a real-data vision system with a data engine and verified edge deployment (months 5–7).** Pick a problem with *your own* data that fits an ECE profile and has Indian relevance. Examples: PCB or solder-joint defect inspection photographed on a lab bench, pothole or road-damage detection from phone video on local roads, or meter and display reading. Use SAM 3 or Grounding DINO to auto-label. Hand-verify a stratified subset and *measure* how often the auto-labels are wrong. Train a small detector or segmenter by distilling from the foundation model. Split the data by capture session or location so related frames cannot leak between train and test. Report per-slice failures. Then export to ONNX, run FP16 and INT8 (TensorRT on a Jetson or a GPU, or ONNX Runtime on CPU), and publish a latency, accuracy and size table, including whether the requested precision actually ran. This project combines the commoditised step (training a detector) with three scarce ones: data-engine QA, evaluation design, and verified low-precision deployment. It hits the deployment cluster that 6/11 postings list. It is also built to survive a project deep-dive.

**Project 2, geometry from your own camera, with C++ (months 9–11).** Calibrate, stitch, then build two-view SfM / visual odometry. Port the RANSAC-homography core to C++. Finish by comparing your pipeline with VGGT-style feed-forward reconstruction and documenting where each fails. This is the "geometry combined with learning" scarce tier. It covers multi-view geometry (6/11 postings) and C++ (6/11). It rehearses the exact Mujin-style interview questions. And it uses Szeliski where the ECE background (sampling, Fourier, linear systems) gives the student a head start over CS peers.

**Project 3, chosen at Gate B (months 13–18).** On the engineer track, aim for a meaningful contribution to a CV or deployment open-source project (OpenCV, ONNX Runtime, timm, or similar), or port Project 1 to a Qualcomm or Jetson NPU with profiling. NVIDIA's NCG postings name open source and performance work as evidence, and edge roles list NPU optimisation. On the research track, reproduce a recent CVPR/ICCV paper with ablations under a mentor, aiming at an arXiv report or workshop paper. True Anomaly asks for paper implementation, and pre-doc, RF and PhD committees read exactly this output. Generative models (Prince 14–18) and VLA or robot learning are specialisms to pick *after* this point, not before. Their hiring is concentrated and mostly at the MS/PhD level.

### Decision gates and the job search running alongside

**Gate A (end of Apr 2027).** Pass criteria: CS231n A1–A2 complete, Project 1 v1 has an honest evaluation report, and the student can solve LeetCode Mediums in about 30 minutes about half the time. If behind, cut stretch chapters, not the project. **Gate B (end of Sep 2027): choose a track.** Choose research only if three things are true: you have found a faculty or industry mentor willing to write a letter, the open-ended debugging in Project 2 energised rather than drained you, and you can accept a 5–7-year horizon. Otherwise, choose engineer. That is the default the market evidence supports.

**Job search, in parallel throughout.** In the current 2026–27 campus season, sit every relevant process now, including services specialist tracks (Infosys SP ₹10–21 LPA; TCS Digital/Prime). The LeetCode practice from month 1 serves these too. A ₹7–10 LPA offer is a reasonable floor, not a failure. If you accept one, the plan continues at 10–12 hours a week, and the realistic target becomes an internal or external move into an ML/CV role at months 12–18. From month 5, apply off-campus to Bengaluru- and Hyderabad-based edge-CV startups, semiconductor/edge-AI GCCs and imaging startups, of the Recrew AI kind, using the GitHub as the application. This follows from the evidence that most AI openings screen on proof of real project work ([JobRise](https://jobrise.io/en/blog/ai-ml-jobs-india-2026/), blog) and that Bengaluru and Hyderabad hold the largest and fastest-growing shares. Do **not** spend effort on US defense or space roles (citizenship-gated) or US big-company "new grad" ML roles (mostly MS/PhD-gated in the sample). Check four things on the official pages, because the notes could not confirm them: the GDM India pre-doc 2027–28 cycle (historically closes in December), MSR India RF timing and whether your department will nominate you, SRFP 2027 eligibility for a graduating student (form reportedly closes 30 Nov 2026, [talentd](https://www.talentd.in/articles/science-academies-summer-research-fellowship)), and GATE/IISc/IIT research-degree calendars.

## Conclusion

Two years ago, "becoming an ML engineer" meant learning to train models. By October 2026, foundation models and coding assistants do much of that. What employers still cannot buy off the shelf is an engineer who can prove what a model does on real data and real hardware, and who understands the camera and geometry underneath it. That shift favours this student more than it first appears. An ECE/EEE background in sampling, filtering, linear systems and hardware constraints maps onto the scarce end of CV: imaging, geometry, edge deployment and measurement. Generic LLM-app work, by contrast, is crowded. The squeeze on juniors is real and measured. It is a squeeze on *unproven* juniors, so 18 months of compounding, verifiable artefacts (a measured evaluation, a latency table, a C++ geometry module, an explained failure) is the most direct response the evidence supports.

The biggest uncertainties are external. They are whether US OPT and H-1B changes stick (watch the OPT rule after the 9 Nov 2026 comment close, and the Supreme Court path on the $100K fee), whether India's AI-posting growth keeps slowing from +49% toward the low double digits, and whether humanoid capital turns into broad hiring or stays inside a few labs. The plan holds up under all three. Its core skills (DL fundamentals, geometry, verified deployment, evaluation) are useful whether the student ends up at a Bengaluru GCC, in an Indian pre-doc programme leading to a funded PhD, or at a robotics startup, and none of them depends on a visa outcome. The one way to fail is to optimise for signals the evidence says are weak, such as course certificates, tutorial-clone repos or mid-table Kaggle ranks, at the expense of the two deep projects an interviewer will spend 45 minutes taking apart.
