Prerequisites

Everything Lectures 23–25 assume you already know

0.1 The square law, with signs

Kang & Leblebici notation, which your notes use: $V_{T0,n} > 0$, $V_{T0,p} < 0$, and the strength of a transistor is $k = \mu C_{ox}\,W/L$. The PMOS uses the same formulas as the NMOS. Its voltages are just all negative.

RegionNMOS (all positive)PMOS (all negative)
Cut-off$V_{GS} \lt V_{T0,n}$, $I_D = 0$$V_{GS} \gt V_{T0,p}$, $I_D = 0$
Linear$V_{DS} \lt V_{GS}-V_{T0,n}$$V_{DS} \gt V_{GS}-V_{T0,p}$
$I_D = \dfrac{k}{2}\left[2(V_{GS}-V_{T0})V_{DS} - V_{DS}^2\right]$
Saturation$V_{DS} \ge V_{GS}-V_{T0,n}$$V_{DS} \le V_{GS}-V_{T0,p}$
$I_D = \dfrac{k}{2}(V_{GS}-V_{T0})^2$
The PMOS inequality flip

For the PMOS, every "$\ge$" of the NMOS becomes "$\le$", because the voltages are negative. If that feels slippery, work in magnitudes: PMOS is saturated when $|V_{DS}| \ge |V_{GS}| - |V_{T0,p}|$, exactly like the NMOS.

0.2 Critical voltages and noise margins

The voltage transfer characteristic (VTC) plots $V_{out}$ against $V_{in}$. Five numbers describe it:

  • $V_{OH}$ and $V_{OL}$: the output high and low levels.
  • $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$: the two inputs where the slope is $dV_{out}/dV_{in} = -1$.
  • $V_{th}$: the switching threshold, where $V_{out} = V_{in}$.
Memorise

$NM_L = V_{IL} - V_{OL}\qquad\qquad NM_H = V_{OH} - V_{IH}$

Why the slope is −1 at the edges

A small noise $\Delta$ on the input appears at the output as $|dV_{out}/dV_{in}|\cdot\Delta$. Where $|$slope$| \lt 1$, the noise shrinks. Where $|$slope$| \gt 1$, it grows. $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$ mark where one regime turns into the other.

0.3 The two pieces of maths you need

(a) Implicit differentiation (Lecture 23)

In the VTC, $V_{out}$ is a function of $V_{in}$. So when you differentiate a current equation with respect to $V_{in}$, every $V_{out}$ term picks up a factor $dV_{out}/dV_{in}$ (the chain rule):

$$\frac{d}{dV_{in}}(V_{out}-V_{DD})^2 = 2(V_{out}-V_{DD})\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}},\qquad \frac{d}{dV_{in}}\Big[(V_{in}-a)V_{out}\Big] = V_{out} + (V_{in}-a)\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}}$$

Then you substitute $dV_{out}/dV_{in} = -1$.

(b) Two integrals (Lecture 25)

$$\int dV = V \qquad\text{and}\qquad \int\frac{dV}{V(a-V)} = \frac{1}{a}\ln\frac{V}{a-V}\quad\left(\text{since } \frac{1}{V(a-V)} = \frac{1}{a}\left[\frac{1}{V}+\frac{1}{a-V}\right]\right)$$

Part 1 · Lecture 23

The CMOS inverter VTC

Two complementary switches, five regions, and the slope −1 derivations

1.1 The circuit

Reading the terminal voltages off the figure is the first step of every CMOS question. Each one translates a transistor condition into a condition on $V_{in}$ and $V_{out}$:

$$V_{GS,n} = V_{in},\quad V_{DS,n} = V_{out},\qquad V_{GS,p} = V_{in}-V_{DD} = -(V_{DD}-V_{in}),\quad V_{DS,p} = V_{out}-V_{DD} = -(V_{DD}-V_{out})$$

1.2 The two saturation boundaries

1
NMOS saturated needs $V_{GS,n} \ge V_{T0,n}$, i.e. $V_{in} \ge V_{T0,n}$. It also needs $V_{DS,n} \ge V_{GS,n} - V_{T0,n}$: $$\boxed{V_{out} \ge V_{in} - V_{T0,n}}\quad\text{(NMOS sat. above this line)}$$
2
PMOS saturated needs $V_{GS,p} \le V_{T0,p}$, i.e. $V_{in} - V_{DD} \le V_{T0,p}$, i.e. $V_{in} \le V_{DD} + V_{T0,p}$. It also needs $V_{DS,p} \le V_{GS,p} - V_{T0,p}$: $$V_{out} - \cancel{V_{DD}} \le V_{in} - \cancel{V_{DD}} - V_{T0,p}\;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{V_{out} \le V_{in} - V_{T0,p}}\quad\text{(PMOS sat. below this line)}$$

Both boundaries are straight lines of slope 1. The NMOS line crosses the axis at $V_{in} = V_{T0,n}$. The PMOS line sits $|V_{T0,p}|$ higher. Draw them on the $(V_{in}, V_{out})$ plane and the VTC passes through every region:

1.3 The five regions

PointInput rangeNMOSPMOSWhat you get
A$V_{in} \lt V_{T0,n}$cut-offlinear$V_{OH} = V_{DD}$ (no current, so no drop across the PMOS)
B$V_{T0,n} \le V_{in} \lt V_{th}$saturationlinear$V_{IL}$ lies here
C$V_{in} = V_{th}$saturationsaturation$V_{th}$ (Lecture 24)
D$V_{th} \lt V_{in} \le V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}$linearsaturation$V_{IH}$ lies here
E$V_{in} \gt V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}$linearcut-off$V_{OL} = 0$
Memory trick

Walk from left to right: the transistor that is turning on is saturated, and the one that is holding the output near its rail is linear. In B, the NMOS is just waking up (saturated) while the PMOS still holds $V_{out}$ near $V_{DD}$ (linear). D is the mirror image.

1.4 Deriving $V_{IL}$ (point B: NMOS sat., PMOS linear)

1
Equate the currents, $I_{D,n} = I_{D,p}$. Use the saturation formula for the NMOS and the linear formula for the PMOS, then divide by $k_p/2$ with $k_R = k_n/k_p$: $$k_R(V_{in}-V_{T0,n})^2 = 2\underbrace{(V_{in}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p})}_{V_{GS,p}-V_{T0,p}}\underbrace{(V_{out}-V_{DD})}_{V_{DS,p}} - (V_{out}-V_{DD})^2 \qquad ①$$
2
Differentiate with respect to $V_{in}$ (the chain rule from §0.3) and cancel the common 2: $$k_R(V_{in}-V_{T0,n}) = (V_{in}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p})\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}} + (V_{out}-V_{DD}) - (V_{out}-V_{DD})\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}}$$
3
Set $V_{in} = V_{IL}$ and $dV_{out}/dV_{in} = -1$: $$k_R(V_{IL}-V_{T0,n}) = -V_{IL}+V_{DD}+V_{T0,p} + V_{out}-V_{DD} + V_{out}-V_{DD}$$
4
Collect the $V_{IL}$ terms on the left, $(1+k_R)V_{IL}$, and divide:
$\displaystyle V_{IL} = \frac{2V_{out} + V_{T0,p} - V_{DD} + k_RV_{T0,n}}{1+k_R}\qquad ②$
5
Solve ① and ② together. Rearrange ② for $V_{out}$, substitute it into ①, and you get a quadratic in $V_{IL}$. Keep the root that lies between $V_{T0,n}$ and $V_{th}$. $$V_{out}\big|_{V_{IL}} = \tfrac12\left[(1+k_R)V_{IL} + V_{DD} - V_{T0,p} - k_RV_{T0,n}\right]$$

1.5 Deriving $V_{IH}$ (point D: NMOS linear, PMOS sat.)

1
Equate the currents with the roles swapped: $$k_R\left[2(V_{in}-V_{T0,n})V_{out} - V_{out}^2\right] = (V_{in}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p})^2 \qquad ③$$
2
Differentiate and cancel the 2s: $$k_R\left[(V_{in}-V_{T0,n})\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}} + V_{out} - V_{out}\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}}\right] = V_{in}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p}$$
3
Set $V_{in} = V_{IH}$ and slope $=-1$: $\;k_R\left[-V_{IH}+V_{T0,n}+2V_{out}\right] = V_{IH}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p}$
4
Collect the terms: $k_R(2V_{out}+V_{T0,n}) + V_{DD}+V_{T0,p} = V_{IH}(1+k_R)$, so
$\displaystyle V_{IH} = \frac{V_{DD} + V_{T0,p} + k_R(2V_{out}+V_{T0,n})}{1+k_R}\qquad ④$
5
Solve ③ and ④ together for $V_{IH}$ and $V_{out}\big|_{V_{IH}}$.
Worked example 23.1 · the lecture method, start to finish

Take $V_{DD} = 5$ V, $V_{T0,n} = 1$ V, $V_{T0,p} = -1$ V, $k_n = 200$ and $k_p = 100\;\mu$A/V², so $k_R = 2$.

$V_{IL}$. From ②, $V_{out} = \tfrac12[3V_{IL} + 5 + 1 - 2] = 1.5V_{IL}+2$, so $V_{out}-5 = 1.5V_{IL}-3$. Substitute into ① and write $x = V_{IL}$:

$$2(x-1)^2 = 2(x-4)(1.5x-3) - (1.5x-3)^2 = (1.5x-3)(0.5x-5)$$ $$2x^2-4x+2 = 0.75x^2 - 9x + 15\;\Rightarrow\;1.25x^2 + 5x - 13 = 0$$ $$x = \frac{-5+\sqrt{90}}{2.5} = \mathbf{1.795\ V},\qquad V_{out} = 1.5x+2 = 4.692\text{ V}$$

$V_{IH}$. From ④, $V_{IH} = (6+4V_{out})/3$, so $V_{out} = 0.75V_{IH} - 1.5$. Substitute into ③:

$$2\left[2(x-1)(0.75x-1.5) - (0.75x-1.5)^2\right] = (x-4)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; 0.875x^2 + 3.5x - 14.5 = 0\;\Rightarrow\; x = \mathbf{2.536\ V}$$

The margins are $NM_L = V_{IL} = 1.795$ V and $NM_H = 5 - 2.536 = 2.464$ V. The stronger NMOS pulls both $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$ to the left.

Shortcut worth knowing (it checks your quadratic)

Eliminating $V_{out}$ by hand gives $V_{IL}$, $V_{IH}$ and $V_{th}$ all in one shape:

$$V_x = \frac{V_{T0,n} + \rho\,(V_{DD}+V_{T0,p})}{1+\rho},\qquad \rho_{th} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k_R}},\quad \rho_{IL} = \frac{2\sqrt{k_R(k_R+3)}-k_R}{k_R(k_R+4)},\quad \rho_{IH} = \frac{1+2\sqrt{1+3k_R}}{3k_R}$$

Here $\rho$ measures how far you have moved from $V_{T0,n}$ toward $V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}$. With $k_R=1$ you get $\rho = 1,\ 3/5,\ 5/3$, which gives the symmetric results of Lecture 24. For $k_R = 2$: $\rho_{IL} = 0.6180$ and $V_{IL} = (1+0.618\times4)/1.618 = 1.795$ V ✓. In an exam, show the lecture method. Use the shortcut to check your answer.

Part 2 · Lecture 24

Switching threshold, sizing and the symmetric inverter

One square root gives $V_{th}$. Run it backwards to size the transistors.

2.1 Deriving $V_{th}$ (point C: both saturated)

1
Equate the currents, both saturated: $$\frac{k_n}{2}(V_{GS,n}-V_{T0,n})^2 = \frac{k_p}{2}(V_{GS,p}-V_{T0,p})^2 \;\Rightarrow\; k_R(V_{in}-V_{T0,n})^2 = (V_{in}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p})^2$$
2
Put $V_{in} = V_{th}$ and take square roots: $\sqrt{k_R}\,(V_{th}-V_{T0,n}) = \pm(V_{th}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p})$.

Which sign? The left side is positive (the NMOS is on). Inside the bracket, $V_{th}-V_{DD}-V_{T0,p} = V_{GS,p}-V_{T0,p} \lt 0$ (the PMOS is on). So take the minus sign: $\sqrt{k_R}(V_{th}-V_{T0,n}) = V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}-V_{th}$.

3
Collect the $V_{th}$ terms: $V_{th}(1+\sqrt{k_R}) = V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}+\sqrt{k_R}V_{T0,n}$. Divide top and bottom by $\sqrt{k_R}$:
$\displaystyle V_{th} = \frac{V_{T0,n} + \sqrt{1/k_R}\,(V_{DD}+V_{T0,p})}{1+\sqrt{1/k_R}}$
Read the formula as a weighted average

$V_{th}$ always lies between $V_{T0,n}$ (where the NMOS starts to turn on) and $V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}$ (where the PMOS turns off). The weight on the PMOS end is $\sqrt{1/k_R}$. As $k_R\to\infty$, $V_{th}\to V_{T0,n}$. As $k_R\to 0$, $V_{th}\to V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}$.

2.2 Design: run it backwards

Go back to step 2 and solve for $k_R$ instead:

$$\sqrt{k_R} = \frac{V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}-V_{th}}{V_{th}-V_{T0,n}}\qquad\xrightarrow{\;V_{th}=V_{DD}/2\;}\qquad \boxed{k_R = \left(\frac{0.5V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}}{0.5V_{DD}-V_{T0,n}}\right)^2}$$

If $V_{T0,n} = |V_{T0,p}|$, the bracket is 1, so $k_R = 1$. Now expand $k_R$. With equal $t_{ox}$, $C_{ox} = \varepsilon_{ox}/t_{ox}$ cancels:

$$\frac{k_n}{k_p} = \frac{\mu_nC_{ox}(W/L)_n}{\mu_pC_{ox}(W/L)_p} = 1\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{(W/L)_n}{(W/L)_p} = \frac{\mu_p}{\mu_n} = \frac{230}{580}\;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{(W/L)_p \approx 2.5\,(W/L)_n}$$
Symmetric inverter

$V_{T0,n} = |V_{T0,p}|$ and $k_R = 1$ give $V_{th} = V_{DD}/2$, and the PMOS is about 2.5× wider to make up for its lower hole mobility.

2.3 The symmetric inverter's $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$

Put $k_R = 1$ and $V_{T0,p} = -V_{T0,n} = -V_T$ into ②. The thresholds cancel:

$$V_{IL} = \frac{2V_{out} - V_T - V_{DD} + V_T}{2} = V_{out} - 0.5V_{DD}\quad\Rightarrow\quad V_{out}-V_{DD} = V_{IL}-0.5V_{DD}$$

The lecture then writes "substitute into ①" and jumps straight to the answer. Here is the algebra in between. Write $x = V_{IL}$:

1
① becomes $(x-V_T)^2 = 2(x-V_{DD}+V_T)(x-\tfrac{V_{DD}}{2}) - (x-\tfrac{V_{DD}}{2})^2$.
2
Expand both sides. The $x^2$ terms cancel: $$x^2 - 2V_Tx + V_T^2 = x^2 - 2V_{DD}x + 2V_Tx + \tfrac34V_{DD}^2 - V_TV_{DD}$$
3
Collect: $x(2V_{DD}-4V_T) = \tfrac34V_{DD}^2 - V_TV_{DD} - V_T^2 = \tfrac14(3V_{DD}+2V_T)(V_{DD}-2V_T)$.
4
Cancel $(V_{DD}-2V_T)$: $V_{IL} = \tfrac18(3V_{DD}+2V_T)$. The same steps on ③ and ④ give $V_{IH} = 0.5V_{DD}+V_{out}$ and $V_{IH} = \tfrac18(5V_{DD}-2V_T)$.
Symmetric results
$$V_{IL} = \frac{3V_{DD}+2V_T}{8}$$ $$V_{IH} = \frac{5V_{DD}-2V_T}{8}$$ $$V_{IL}+V_{IH} = V_{DD}$$ $$NM_L = NM_H = V_{IL}$$
Remember "3 + 5 = 8"

Low gets 3, high gets 5, both over 8. The $\pm2V_T$ signs make the pair add to $V_{DD}$.

Why the margins are equal: $NM_L = V_{IL} - 0$, and $NM_H = V_{DD} - V_{IH} = V_{DD} - (V_{DD}-V_{IL}) = V_{IL}$.

2.4 Layout

One vertical poly line is the common gate ($V_{in}$). Where it crosses the p⁺ region (inside the n-well) it forms the PMOS. Where it crosses the n⁺ region it forms the NMOS.

The sources contact the $V_{DD}$ and GND rails. A metal strap joins the two drains to form $V_{out}$.

The p⁺ region is drawn about 2.5× wider. That is §2.2's sizing made visible.

2.2 Equal delays need equal strengths, so $(W/L)_p = 3(W/L)_n$, giving $k_R = 1$ with matched thresholds: the inverter is symmetric. $V_{IL} = (7.5+1.2)/8 = 1.0875$ V and $V_{IH} = (12.5-1.2)/8 = 1.4125$ V. $NM_L = NM_H = 1.0875$ V and $V_{th} = 1.25$ V.
2.3 $k_R = \left(\dfrac{1.65-0.7}{1.65-0.6}\right)^2 = (0.9048)^2 = 0.8186$. Then $\dfrac{(W/L)_p}{(W/L)_n} = \dfrac{\mu_n}{\mu_p\,k_R} = \dfrac{2.522}{0.8186} =$ 3.08. The larger $|V_{T0,p}|$ weakens the PMOS, so it must be made even wider than 2.5×.
2.4 $V_{th} = \dfrac{1 + 0.5\times4}{1.5} = 2.0$ V. The shortcut gives $V_{IL} = 1.512$ V and $V_{IH} = 2.219$ V, so $NM_L = 1.51$ V and $NM_H = 2.78$ V. They are no longer equal: $NM_H$ grows while $NM_L$ shrinks from 1.75 V. The symmetric design maximises the worse of the two margins.
2.5 In the symmetric case the PMOS is the NMOS with every voltage measured from the other rail. So if $(V_{in}, V_{out})$ lies on the VTC, so does $(V_{DD}-V_{in}, V_{DD}-V_{out})$. That map sends a slope of −1 to a slope of −1, so it carries the point $V_{IL}$ to the point $V_{IH}$. Therefore $V_{IH} = V_{DD}-V_{IL}$.
Part 3 · Lecture 25

Switching characteristics and delay

The output is a capacitor. The transistors are the currents that charge and discharge it.

3.1 Where the load capacitance comes from

Lumped load

$C_{load} = C_{gd,p} + C_{gd,n} + C_{db,p} + C_{db,n} + C_{int} + C_g$

$C_{gd}$ is gate–drain overlap. $C_{db}$ is the drain–bulk junction. $C_{int}$ is the wire. $C_g$ is the next stage's gate capacitance: $C_g \approx C_{ox}\left[(WL)_n + (WL)_p\right]$.

From Razavi §2.4.2: $C_{gd} = WC_{ov}\;(= C_{ox}WL_D)$. The junction capacitance is $C_{db} = C_j\cdot(\text{drain area}) + C_{jsw}\cdot(\text{drain perimeter})$. A folded layout (Razavi Ex. 2.10) halves the drain area and so shrinks $C_{db}$.

3.2 The lumped model

All the physics collapses to one node equation (KCL at $V_{out}$):

$$i_C = i_{D,p} - i_{D,n} = C_{load}\frac{dV_{out}}{dt}$$

Falling output ($V_{in}$ steps high): the PMOS turns off, so $i_C = -i_{D,n}$ and the NMOS discharges $C_{load}$.

Rising output ($V_{in}$ steps low): the NMOS turns off, so the PMOS charges $C_{load}$.

3.3 Definitions

QuantityDefinition
$V_{50\%}$$V_{OL} + \tfrac12(V_{OH}-V_{OL}) = \dfrac{V_{OH}+V_{OL}}{2}$
$\tau_{PHL}$ (output high→low)$t_2 - t_1$
$\tau_{PLH}$ (output low→high)$t_4 - t_3$
$\tau_P$ (average)$\dfrac{\tau_{PHL}+\tau_{PLH}}{2}$

3.4 Method 1: average current

Pretend the capacitor current is constant at its average value. Then $i = C\,dV/dt$ becomes $\tau = C\,\Delta V/I_{avg}$:

$$\tau_{PHL} = \frac{C_{load}\,\Delta V_{HL}}{I_{avg,HL}},\qquad \Delta V_{HL} = V_{OH}-V_{50\%} = \frac{V_{OH}-V_{OL}}{2}$$ $$I_{avg,HL} = \tfrac12\left[i_C(V_{in}=V_{OH},\,V_{out}=V_{OH}) + i_C(V_{in}=V_{OH},\,V_{out}=V_{50\%})\right]$$

Likewise $\tau_{PLH} = C_{load}\Delta V_{LH}/I_{avg,LH}$, with $\Delta V_{LH} = V_{50\%}-V_{OL}$, and the current averaged at $V_{out} = V_{OL}$ and $V_{out} = V_{50\%}$ (with $V_{in} = V_{OL}$).

Pick the right region for each endpoint

Consider the falling output. At its start ($V_{out} = V_{OH}$), $V_{DS,n} = V_{OH} \ge V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}$, so the NMOS is saturated. At $V_{50\%}$, normally $V_{50\%} \lt V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}$, so it is linear. Using the saturation formula at both ends is the most common mistake.

3.5 Method 2: state equations (exact integration)

Step the input from $V_{OL}$ to $V_{OH}$ at $t_1$. As $V_{out}$ falls, the NMOS passes through two regions, so the integral splits in two:

Segment 1: saturated, $V_{OH} \to V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}$ (in the lecture)

The NMOS stays saturated while $V_{out} \ge V_{in}-V_{T0,n} = V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}$. Its current is the constant $\tfrac{k_n}{2}(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})^2$:

$$dt = -\frac{C_{load}\,dV_{out}}{i_{D,n}}\;\Rightarrow\; \int_{t_1}^{t_1'}dt = \frac{-C_{load}}{\frac{k_n}{2}(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})^2}\int_{V_{OH}}^{V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}}dV_{out}\;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{t_1'-t_1 = \frac{2C_{load}V_{T0,n}}{k_n(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})^2}}$$

Segment 2: linear, $V_{OH}-V_{T0,n} \to V_{50\%}$ (completing the lecture)

Now $i_{D,n} = \tfrac{k_n}{2}\left[2(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})V_{out}-V_{out}^2\right]$. Write $a = 2(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})$, so the bracket is $V_{out}(a-V_{out})$:

$$t_2 - t_1' = -\frac{2C_{load}}{k_n}\int_{V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}}^{V_{50\%}}\frac{dV_{out}}{V_{out}(a-V_{out})} = \frac{2C_{load}}{k_n\,a}\left[\ln\frac{V}{a-V}\right]_{V_{50\%}}^{V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}}$$

At the upper limit $V = a/2$, so $\ln\frac{V}{a-V} = \ln 1 = 0$. That leaves

$$t_2 - t_1' = \frac{C_{load}}{k_n(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})}\ln\!\left(\frac{2(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})}{V_{50\%}}-1\right)$$
Memorise: exact step-input delays
$$\tau_{PHL} = \frac{C_{load}}{k_n(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})}\left[\frac{2V_{T0,n}}{V_{OH}-V_{T0,n}} + \ln\!\left(\frac{4(V_{OH}-V_{T0,n})}{V_{OH}+V_{OL}}-1\right)\right]$$ $$\tau_{PLH} = \frac{C_{load}}{k_p(V_{OH}-V_{OL}-|V_{T0,p}|)}\left[\frac{2|V_{T0,p}|}{V_{OH}-V_{OL}-|V_{T0,p}|} + \ln\!\left(\frac{4(V_{OH}-V_{OL}-|V_{T0,p}|)}{V_{OH}-V_{OL}}-1\right)\right]$$

For CMOS ($V_{OH}=V_{DD}$, $V_{OL}=0$), both have the form $\dfrac{C}{k\,(V_{DD}-|V_T|)}\Big[\tfrac{2|V_T|}{V_{DD}-|V_T|} + \ln\!\big(\tfrac{4(V_{DD}-|V_T|)}{V_{DD}}-1\big)\Big]$. The PLH formula is the PHL one with $n\to p$ and voltages measured down from $V_{DD}$.

Worked example 25.2

Take $V_{DD} = 3.3$ V, $C_{load} = 0.3$ pF, $k_n = 200$ and $k_p = 80\;\mu$A/V², $V_{T0,n} = 0.6$ V, $V_{T0,p} = -0.7$ V.

Average current, HL. At $V_{out}=3.3$ (sat.): $100\,\mu(2.7)^2 = 729\;\mu$A. At $V_{out}=1.65$ (lin.): $100\,\mu[2(2.7)(1.65)-1.65^2] = 619\;\mu$A. The average is $674\;\mu$A, so $\tau_{PHL} = 0.3\text{p}\times1.65/674\,\mu = \mathbf{0.734}$ ns.

Exact, HL. $\dfrac{0.3\text{p}}{200\mu\times2.7} = 0.556$ ns, times $\left[\tfrac{1.2}{2.7} + \ln\!\big(\tfrac{10.8}{3.3}-1\big)\right] = 0.444+0.821$, gives $\tau_{PHL} = 0.247 + 0.456 = \mathbf{0.703}$ ns.

Exact, LH. $\dfrac{0.3\text{p}}{80\mu\times2.6}\left[\tfrac{1.4}{2.6}+\ln\!\big(\tfrac{10.4}{3.3}-1\big)\right] = 1.442\times(0.538+0.766)$, so $\tau_{PLH} = \mathbf{1.88}$ ns (the average-current method gives 1.96 ns). Then $\tau_P = 1.29$ ns.

The average-current method overestimates by about 4 %. That is good enough for most questions, but use whichever method the question names.

3.6 What the formulas tell a designer

Both delays have the form $\tau \propto \dfrac{C_{load}}{k\,(V_{DD}-|V_T|)}$. Razavi's one-line estimate (§17.1) is $T_d \approx C V_{DD}/I$. To go faster:

  • Reduce $C_{load}$: shorter wires, smaller fan-out, folded drains.
  • Increase $k$: make $W/L$ larger. But wider devices add their own $C_{db}$ and load the previous stage more, so the gain is limited.
  • Increase $V_{DD}$: faster, but power $\propto CV_{DD}^2f$ rises.

Equal rise and fall need $k_p = k_n$ (with matched thresholds). That is the same $(W/L)_p \approx 2.5(W/L)_n$ as Lecture 24's symmetric inverter, which is why Compre Q4 said "sized for equal delays" and meant "symmetric".

The plot drives an identical next stage, so a wider PMOS also adds load capacitance. $\tau_{PLH}$ falls as the PMOS widens, but $\tau_{PHL}$ rises (more capacitance, same NMOS).

The delays are equal at $\beta \approx 2.7$. The average is smallest a little earlier, at $\beta\approx2.3$ (black dot). Equal edges and minimum average delay are slightly different goals.

Memory tricks

Regions A–E

"Off-Lin, Sat-Lin, Sat-Sat, Lin-Sat, Lin-Off", NMOS first. The middle three spell a palindrome around C.

V_IL vs V_IH formulas

Both have $1+k_R$ below and $2V_{out}$ on top. In $V_{IL}$, $k_R$ multiplies only $V_{T0,n}$. In $V_{IH}$, it multiplies $(2V_{out}+V_{T0,n})$, because the NMOS now sets the output.

V_th as a tug-of-war

It sits between $V_{T0,n}$ and $V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}$. The stronger side pulls $V_{th}$ toward its own threshold.

Delay

"$C$ over $kV$, times [sat. bit + log bit]." The sat. bit is $2V_T/(V_{DD}-V_T)$. The log bit is $\ln(4\cdot\text{overdrive}/V_{DD}-1)$.

Exam playbook

  1. Any CMOS VTC question: compute $k_n = \mu_nC_{ox}(W/L)_n$, $k_p$ and $k_R$ first. Write the four terminal voltages, then $V_{th}$ from the boxed formula.
  2. $V_{IL}$ / $V_{IH}$: write ① or ③, differentiate, set the slope to −1, solve for $V_{out}$ in terms of $V_{IL}$ (or $V_{IH}$), substitute, and solve the quadratic. Check the answer with the $\rho$ shortcut. If the inverter is symmetric, use the $3/8$ and $5/8$ forms directly.
  3. Design for $V_{th}$: $k_R = \left(\frac{V_{DD}+V_{T0,p}-V_{th}}{V_{th}-V_{T0,n}}\right)^2$, then convert to $W/L$ using the mobility ratio. "Equal rise/fall" means symmetric.
  4. Delay: write $C_{load}$, decide each transistor's region at both endpoints, then use average current or the exact formula, whichever the question asks for. Always state $V_{50\%}$.
