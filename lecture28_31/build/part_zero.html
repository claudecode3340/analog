Z.3 The MOSFET in one paragraph

A MOSFET has a gate (G), a source (S) and a drain (D). The gate voltage opens or closes a channel between source and drain. It turns on when the gate–source voltage passes the threshold $V_T$ (NMOS: $V_{GS} > V_{T0,n}$; PMOS: $V_{GS} < V_{T0,p}$, which is negative). How much current it carries is set by $k = \mu C_{ox}\,(W/L)$: a wider ($W$) or shorter ($L$) transistor gives more current.

Z.4 The inverter and its switching threshold (Lectures 23–24)

The one formula Lecture 28 borrows $$V_{th} = \frac{V_{T0,n} + \sqrt{1/k_R}\,(V_{DD} + V_{T0,p})}{1 + \sqrt{1/k_R}},\qquad k_R = \frac{k_n}{k_p} = \frac{\mu_nC_{ox}(W/L)_n}{\mu_pC_{ox}(W/L)_p}$$ Stronger NMOS (bigger $k_R$) → $V_{th}$ moves down. Stronger PMOS → $V_{th}$ moves up.
