R7 · VOL of a ratioed (resistor-load) inverter (Razavi §3.3.1) In lectures

A resistor-load inverter (Lecture 37's first ratioed gate; Razavi Fig. 3.4) has $V_{DD} = 3$ V, $R_D = 2$ kΩ and an NMOS with $\mu_nC_{ox}W/L = 1343$ µA/V², $V_{TH} = 0.7$ V. Estimate $V_{OL}$ for $V_{in} = 3$ V with Razavi's deep-triode formula, then compare with the exact square-law value.

In plain words
The resistor and the ON transistor form a voltage divider. How low can the output get?
Theory
Razavi Eq. 3.6–3.7: $V_{out} = V_{DD}\frac{R_{on}}{R_{on}+R_D}$, $R_{on} = \frac{1}{\mu_nC_{ox}\frac WL(V_{in}-V_{TH})}$.
Plan
$R_{on}$ → divider → compare with the exact triode equation $\frac{V_{DD}-V}{R_D} = k[(V_{in}-V_{TH})V - V^2/2]$.
Solve
  1. $R_{on} = \frac{1}{1343\,\mu\times2.3} = 324$ Ω.
  2. $V_{OL} \approx 3\times\frac{324}{324+2000} =$ 0.42 V; exact: 0.46 V.
Check
$V_{OL} > 0$: the defining weakness of ratioed logic ✓. A bigger $R_D$ (weaker load) lowers $V_{OL}$ but slows the rising edge, the same trade-off as pseudo-NMOS.
