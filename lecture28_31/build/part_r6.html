R6 · Velocity saturation vs the square law (Razavi §11.2, §17.2.3) Optional

Lecture 34 lists velocity saturation as a limitation of the delay model. A short NMOS has W = 1 µm, L = 40 nm, $C_{ox} = 3.84$ fF/µm², $\mu_nC_{ox} = 134.3$ µA/V², $v_{sat} = 10^7$ cm/s. Compare the square-law current with the fully velocity-saturated current $I_D = WC_{ox}(V_{GS}-V_{TH})v_{sat}$ for overdrives of 0.3 V and 0.6 V.

In plain words
Does doubling the overdrive really give four times the current in a tiny transistor?
Theory
Square law $I = \frac12\mu C_{ox}\frac WL V_{ov}^2$; velocity saturation $I = WC_{ox}V_{ov}v_{sat}$ (Razavi Eq. 11.1 / 17.25).
Plan
Use SI units: $C_{ox} = 3.84\times10^{-3}$ F/m², $v_{sat} = 10^5$ m/s, $W = 10^{-6}$ m.
Solve
  1. $V_{ov} = 0.3$ V: square law $\frac12\times134.3\,\mu\times25\times0.09 = 151$ µA; saturated $10^{-6}\times3.84\times10^{-3}\times0.3\times10^5 =$ 115 µA.
  2. $V_{ov} = 0.6$ V: square law $604$ µA (×4); saturated 230 µA (×2 only).
Check
The real current is smaller and grows more slowly, so real gates are slower than $d = gh + p$ predicts at high $V_{DD}$, exactly the lecture's point ✓.
